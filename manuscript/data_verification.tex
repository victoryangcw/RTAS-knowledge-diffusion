\subsection{Data verification and numerical provenance}\label{sec:verification}

Before manuscript finalization we conducted a post-freeze numerical provenance audit in three layers. First, after the primary analysis pipeline and the audit scope had been frozen, a separate verification script reloaded the frozen data tables and independently recomputed 118 quantities spanning corpus construction and advisor--article linkage, the RTAS distribution and aggregation-family selection, the frozen validation program, topic and diffusion-lag counts, and the RQ1, RQ3, and RQ4 models. All 118 recomputed values matched the reported frozen values (Online Resource 1, Tables~S3 and S5). Later robustness additions that were created after this 118-check ledger was frozen---including the within-unit-year calibration, the balanced-corpus BERTopic sensitivity, and the hard-pair challenge set---are documented separately and do not change the ledger count. The audit additionally reconstructed every project's RTAS value from the underlying 56,901-article and 3,714-project embedding files, verifying the row alignment of the embedding tables against the frozen datasets and reproducing all 3,713 non-empty portfolio values to within $4\times10^{-8}$---float storage precision---with the single empty-portfolio project confirmed to be stored as zero by convention; together these account for all 3,714 projects. Simulation-based quantities inside the frozen audit scope were reproduced under the frozen seeding and resampling protocols; both the BGE--MiniLM and RQ1 $\eta^2$ bootstrap intervals reproduce exactly. Second, the project side was verified against source records rather than only against the consolidated table: the four official 2021--2024 approval lists were independently re-parsed, reproducing the funding-tier counts, the 1,834-advisor/4,244-link/528-co-supervised rebuild, the 158 entrepreneurship-track projects, and the frame reconciliation from 3,887 registry rows to 3,714 projects (Study frame and registry verification, above). Third, each headline conclusion was re-estimated under alternative samples or specifications---excluding the entrepreneurship-track projects (Table~S1), excluding the structurally different 2022 cohort (Table~S2), leave-advisor-out portfolio construction, the high-confidence advisor-match subset, single-advisor projects only, within academic-unit $\times$ year standardized RTAS, alternative encoders and lexical baselines, alternative topic thresholds, and lagged as well as contemporaneous regression specifications. Primary regression conclusions retained their sign and significance classification throughout; the static topic-quadrant asymmetry, by contrast, proved sensitive to paper/project corpus composition (a 1:1 balanced re-run does not reproduce the full-corpus HRLT excess) and is therefore reported as a composition-dependent descriptive finding rather than a stable structural result.

Several statistics in this paper share a name but differ by construction; they are distinct estimands rather than competing estimates, and Table~S5 lists them side by side. In particular: (i) the RQ1 effect size $\eta^2=1.89\%$ is a project-level one-way analysis of variance across funding tiers ($N=3{,}714$), whereas the $\eta^2$ values entering the aggregation-family comparison (Table~2) were computed on the 12,000-article development pool used to select the portfolio aggregation rule, which was frozen before the full 56,901-article corpus was built; development-pool selection statistics are reported only as selection evidence. (ii) Three variance quantities coexist for colleges: the conditional ICC of 0.7376 from the full mixed model, the unconditional-means-model ICC of 0.7523, and the descriptive one-way college ANOVA effect $\eta^2=66.90\%$; each answers a different question. (iii) The number of colleges is 40 in the study frame, 39 in the descriptive college ANOVA (the single-project unit cannot supply a within-group variance estimate), and 39 in the complete-case mixed model (the same unit, the School of International Education, contributes no complete case because its sole project lacks an advisor pre-project covariate). (iv) The two concentration Gini coefficients use different outcome definitions---national projects with all 1,236 zero-national advisors retained in the headcount denominator (0.746) versus all distinct projects (0.368)---and the two persistence odds ratios use different risk sets, consecutive active advisor--years (2.06) versus the full prior-active advisor--year grid (2.45). (v) The article counts 56,901, 33,312, 60,615, 22,438, and 79,339 refer to five explicitly different pools (the retrieval corpus, the advisor-matched portfolio corpus, the joint BERTopic corpus, the auxiliary 2017--2019 window, and their union) and are never interchangeable.

\section*{Numerical provenance and reconciliation}

After the primary analysis pipeline froze, a verification script independently reloaded the frozen analysis tables and recomputed 118 quantities within the frozen audit scope without reference to the scripts that originally produced the reported values. These quantities span corpus construction and advisor--article linkage, the frozen RTAS distribution, aggregation-family selection, the frozen validation program, topic and diffusion-lag counts, and all RQ1/RQ3/RQ4 estimates; all 118 matched the reported frozen values to printed precision. Later robustness additions created after the 118-check ledger was frozen are reported separately and are not counted in the ledger. The frozen RTAS values were additionally reconstructed row by row from the embedding files: all 3,713 non-empty portfolios agree to within $4\times10^{-8}$, while the remaining project has an empty constructable portfolio and is stored as RTAS $=0$ by convention; together these account for all 3,714 projects. The embedding-table row order was also verified against the frozen datasets. The project registry was additionally verified by re-parsing the four official 2021--2024 approval lists from their source spreadsheets (funding tiers, project-type labels, annual coverage, identifier uniqueness, and the 3,887-row to 3,714-row frame reconciliation), and every headline model was re-estimated under alternative samples and specifications (Tables~S1--S2; main text Sections on sensitivity analyses). Both bootstrap intervals within the frozen audit scope reproduce exactly when the frozen seed and resampling protocol are applied. Tables~S3 and S5 document the canonical audited quantities; Table~S4 reports the post-freeze hard-pair challenge-set robustness outside the 118-check ledger. The complete machine-readable ledger, including the row-level reconstruction checks, accompanies the analysis code.
