We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled “Linking Undergraduate Innovation and Entrepreneurship Training Projects to Advisor-Linked Academic-Unit Publication Portfolios: Cross-Lingual Alignment and the Limited Role of Administrative Tier” for consideration as a Research Article in Scientometrics.

Universities increasingly seek to connect undergraduate training with active faculty research, yet whether student project topics align with the research environment around them is rarely measured directly. We introduce the Research–Training Alignment Score (RTAS), a project-level measure of semantic alignment between student project titles and the publication portfolios of their academic units. RTAS combines cross-lingual sentence embeddings with advisor–author linkage, enabling project records and publication metadata to be compared across languages. We apply the measure to 3,714 undergraduate innovation and entrepreneurship training projects and 56,901 OpenAlex-indexed articles at Wuhan University.

The manuscript evaluates RTAS using human ratings of title pairs together with independent-rater, lexical-baseline, test–retest, and second-encoder checks. The main analysis indicates that alignment is structured predominantly at the academic-unit level (ICC = 0.74). Within units, advisors’ pre-project publication activity is positively associated with RTAS, whereas prior supervisory load is negatively associated; both associations persist after excluding the focal advisors’ own publications from the reference portfolios. By contrast, administrative tier contributes little explanatory value after adjustment. National-level project allocation is concentrated and persistent, but projects linked to the top 5% of advisors by national-project count show no clear alignment advantage. We interpret these findings descriptively rather than causally and emphasize that RTAS measures semantic proximity to a research environment, not research quality, teaching quality, or project performance.

We believe the manuscript is well suited to Scientometrics because its central contribution is methodological. It extends scientometric measurement to an observable dimension of the research–training relationship by combining bibliographic data, advisor–author linkage, cross-lingual semantic embeddings, and multilevel analysis. The framework is intended as a bounded and potentially transferable measure rather than an institutional ranking or a proxy for educational quality.

The manuscript is accompanied by transparent analysis and audit materials. The project repository provides the analysis code, non-identifying derived outputs, validation materials, and numerical provenance records needed to inspect the reported results: https://github.com/victoryangcw/RTAS-knowledge-diffusion. Administrative records containing personal information are not redistributed. The manuscript includes a Data Availability Statement describing the restricted administrative layer; the ethics/data-use statement will be finalized before submission in accordance with the responsible institutional determination.

This manuscript is original and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere. All authors will approve the final manuscript and its submission before submission.
