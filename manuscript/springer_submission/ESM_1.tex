The official 2021--2024 approval lists classify projects as innovation training or entrepreneurship training/practice. Matching on (year, normalized title) identifies 158 unique entrepreneurship projects inside the frozen 3,714-row dataset (4.3\%; 74/23/26/35 across 2021--2024; 53 university-, 64 provincial-, and 41 national-level). These projects have lower alignment than the remaining 3,556 projects: mean RTAS $=0.100$ (SD $=0.053$) versus $0.135$ (SD $=0.073$); Welch $t=-7.90$, $p<0.001$, Cohen $d=-0.49$ (pooled SD). Table~S1 re-estimates the frozen random-intercept specification (REML, clustered by academic unit, covariates taken from the frozen dataset unchanged) after excluding these 158 projects: the complete-case sample falls from 3,231 to 3,085 projects, ICC changes from 0.7376 to 0.7373, every coefficient retains its sign and significance classification, and the administrative-tier indicators remain non-significant. The 2020 cohort is retained in both fits because no type-labeled official registry exists for that year.

\section*{Supplementary Methods: human-rating rubric}

Human raters assessed semantic relatedness using only the project title and article title, without access to RTAS/cosine values or the other rater's score. The same 1--4 anchor definitions were used for the frozen C1 validation and the post-freeze hard-pair challenge set:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{4 --- Directly relevant:} same research problem, or directly supporting method/data.
\item \textbf{3 --- Substantially relevant:} same problem area or methodology domain, with clear overlap.
\item \textbf{2 --- Weakly relevant:} shared broad discipline or generic methods, but different research problems.
\item \textbf{1 --- Not relevant:} no meaningful semantic connection.
\end{itemize}

For the two-rater hard-pair challenge set, the consensus score is the arithmetic mean of the two ordinal ratings; no adjudication was used, and no pair differed by more than one scale point. For binary AUC analyses, consensus $\ge2$ is classified as related and consensus $=1$ as unrelated.

\section*{Numerical provenance and reconciliation}

After the primary analysis pipeline froze, a verification script independently reloaded the frozen analysis tables and recomputed 118 pre-specified primary-analysis quantities without reference to the scripts that originally produced the reported values. These quantities span corpus construction and advisor--article linkage, the frozen RTAS distribution, aggregation-family selection, the frozen human/LLM validity program, topic and diffusion-lag counts, and the RQ1, RQ3, and RQ4 models; all 118 matched the reported frozen values to printed precision. Post-freeze robustness analyses are documented separately and are not part of this 118-check frozen provenance ledger. The frozen RTAS values were additionally reconstructed row by row from the embedding files: all 3,713 non-empty portfolios agree to within $4\times10^{-8}$, while the remaining project has an empty constructable portfolio and is stored as RTAS $=0$ by the frozen convention; together these account for all 3,714 projects. The embedding-table row order was also verified against the frozen datasets. The project registry was additionally verified by re-parsing the four official 2021--2024 approval lists from their source spreadsheets (funding tiers, project-type labels, annual coverage, identifier uniqueness, and the 3,887-row to 3,714-row frame reconciliation), and every headline model was re-estimated under alternative samples and specifications (Tables~S1--S2; main text Sections on sensitivity analyses). Both frozen bootstrap intervals reproduce exactly when the frozen seed and resampling protocol are applied. Tables~S3 and S5 document the canonical audited quantities; Table~S4 reports the post-freeze hard-pair challenge-set robustness outside the 118-check ledger. The complete machine-readable ledger, including the row-level reconstruction checks, accompanies the analysis code.
